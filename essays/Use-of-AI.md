## I. Introduction

AI has become a common part of technical education, and ICS 314 was no different. While AI tools were not required, I regularly used **ChatGPT** throughout the course as part of my workflow. Other tools like GitHub Copilot and Bard exist, but ChatGPT fit best with how I learn and solve problems.

Across the semester, I used AI as a support tool to clarify concepts, interpret error messages, and help me think through problems when I got stuck. This essay reflects on how I used AI in ICS 314 and how it influenced my learning and understanding of software engineering.

---

## II. How I Used AI in ICS 314

I used AI most often during **WODs**, the **final project**, and when learning new tools. For **Experience WODs**, AI helped me get oriented and understand how to approach a problem. For example, during a functional programming WOD, I asked:

> “Write a JavaScript function using underscore.js to implement the following instructions.”

This gave me a rough structure and helped clarify what the WOD was asking, even though the code usually needed changes to fully meet the requirements.  

For **Practice WODs and graded in-class WODs**, I used AI in a more targeted way. It was most helpful for quick checks—confirming syntax, recalling how a function behaved, or understanding why a small piece of code wasn’t working. Under time pressure, this kind of clarification helped me move forward without replacing the need to understand the material.

AI was especially useful during the **final project**, where multiple frameworks and tools interacted. I frequently used it to understand Prisma errors, database migrations, and unexpected Next.js behavior. A common prompt I used was:

> “What does this Prisma error mean, and how can I fix it safely?”

When learning new tools like Prisma, NextAuth, and GitHub Actions, AI also helped by providing high-level explanations that made documentation easier to understand. Instead of just following commands, I could understand why certain steps mattered.

I also used AI for **writing and communication tasks**. For essays, it helped me organize ideas and structure my responses, such as when I asked:

> “Help me create an outline for an effort estimation essay.”

This made it easier to focus on content while ensuring I addressed the prompt clearly. AI also helped me phrase clearer technical questions and explain unfamiliar starter code, which improved communication with teammates and instructors. For documentation, it helped clean up comments so they were easier to read and maintain.

One of the most valuable uses of AI was **debugging and quality assurance**. I often asked questions like:

> “What’s wrong with this code?”  
> “Why is ESLint complaining about this?”

This saved time and reduced frustration, especially during later stages of development.

---

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding

Using AI helped me move past roadblocks faster and stay productive when I was stuck. At the same time, it encouraged me to verify information and think critically, especially when answers were incomplete or unclear. Overall, AI supported my learning without replacing the need to understand core concepts.

---

## IV. Practical Use Beyond ICS 314

Outside of ICS 314, I have used AI in other computer engineering courses for debugging and learning new material. The experience was consistent with this course: AI works best as a support tool alongside traditional learning methods.

---

## V. Challenges, Comparison, and Future Use

One challenge with AI is that responses can sound confident even when they are wrong, which makes verification important. Compared to traditional learning methods, AI-assisted learning is faster and more flexible, while traditional approaches are better for deep understanding. In my experience, combining both led to the best results.

Looking forward, AI will likely continue to play a larger role in software engineering education. Clear guidance on effective and responsible use would help students integrate these tools more thoughtfully.

---

## VI. Conclusion

AI played a supportive role throughout my experience in ICS 314. It helped me understand concepts, debug code, and work more efficiently, especially during the final project. When used intentionally, AI can be a valuable tool for learning and applying software engineering principles.
